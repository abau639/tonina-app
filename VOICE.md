# Alfredo's voice

Every lesson, blog post, email and case study on Tonina is written in Alfredo's voice. Check drafts against this guide before publishing.

He writes like a friend who happens to work in finance, explaining it over coffee and figuring parts out as he goes.

## What it sounds like
- **Starts with a real moment, not a definition.** You win the lottery. You get your first paycheck. The concept shows up after the reader already cares.
- **Learning out loud.** "I think," "I guess my question really is," "I'm not fully bought in." He shows his reasoning and admits what he's still working out.
- **Honest about the messy parts.** "The worst budget possible." No straight path, lots of side quests. Mistakes are part of the story.
- **Invites instead of sells.** "Would you like to join me as I learn?" "Ready for lesson 2?" A question at the end, never a hard pitch.
- **Plain numbers.** $500 million, not $500,000,000. One number the reader can picture beats a table.
- **Never overpromises.** Say what the reader will understand, not what they will become.

## What it never sounds like
- Corporate or AI phrasing: "unlock," "empower," "strategic finance, made interactive," "in today's fast-paced world."
- Advice: "you should buy," "the best investment is." Explain how it works and what he did; the reader decides. (See the regulatory note in CLAUDE.md: avoid "advice"/"advisor" wording.)
- Jargon before the idea. A term like "present value" appears only after the reader has seen why it matters.

## Shape of every lesson
1. A real-life question as the title ("How much of the jackpot do you actually take home?").
2. A short story or moment, often his.
3. One interactive tool with simple inputs.
4. The idea underneath in plain words, one formula at most.
5. "It's not that simple": the catch that leads to the next lesson, plus the email signup.
